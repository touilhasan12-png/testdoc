# CASE BRIEF v1 — THE SODDER CHILDREN

**Working title (English, on screen):** `THE SODDER CHILDREN — Five Who Were Never Found`
**Format:** documentary pilot · ~5:00 · 1080p · English audio and English text only
**Look:** neo-noir archival — charcoal/black/grey/blue-grey/aged paper, restrained red accents,
dramatic single-source lighting, light film grain, halation on highlights.

## The case in one paragraph

On the night of 24–25 December 1945 the home of George and Jennie Sodder in Fayetteville,
West Virginia burned. Four of the ten children got out. Five — Maurice (14), Martha (12),
Louis (9), Jennie (8) and Betty (5) — were never seen again. No bones, no teeth, no remains
of any kind were recovered from the ashes, which the family and later independent observers
found impossible for a fire of that duration. The parents spent the rest of their lives
searching; in 1968 they put up a billboard on Route 16 with the children's photographs.
The case has never been closed and was publicly re-opened as a cold case in 2021.

## Why this case (production rationale)

| Need of the pilot | How this case satisfies it |
|---|---|
| No graphic content | The horror is an absence of evidence, not a crime scene. Nothing to stage, nothing to sanitise |
| Real period clippings | Heavy U.S. newspaper coverage 1945–1968 → Chronicling America and the LoC return genuine pages |
| Public-domain photographs | The family portrait and the 1968 billboard are widely held public-domain images → a legitimate restoration chain with an honest disclosure |
| Handwritten artefacts | The family's letters, the billboard copy, the anonymous 1968 letter and photo → handwriting and manuscript-ageing tools |
| Geography carries the plot | Fayetteville, the house site, Route 16, Charleston, the reported sightings → maps, paths, street imagery |
| Chronology carries the plot | 1945 fire → 1947 inquest → 1949 reward → 1968 billboard → 2021 re-opening → timeline and charts |
| A dense lead network | The telephone operator, the insurance salesman, the travelling salesman, the woman who claimed to have fed the children, the bus/taxi sightings → evidence board and relationship graph |
| Style match | 1940s coal country, snow, a family portrait under a bare bulb — this is the requested palette exactly |

## Ethical frame (rule 6)

- The five children are named once, respectfully, and are never described in terms of suffering.
- No reenactment of any death. Reenactment is limited to empty spaces: a road, a snow field, a telephone, a window.
- Every archival image carries `Archival photo`. Every colourised image carries `Colourised reconstruction`.
  Every manufactured document carries `Re-created`. Every staged space carries `Reenactment`.
- Living descendants are not named, contacted or depicted. No real person's voice is cloned.
- The film states plainly which threads are documented and which are family belief, and never asserts
  a solution.

## Claim discipline (rule 4)

Every factual sentence in the script is bound to a source id in `01_research/claims.json`.
Each source id resolves to (a) an archived capture produced by Auto-Archiver and (b) a
citation line for `CREDITS.md`. A claim without a source id is deleted before the script is
finalised. Targets: Chronicling America, Library of Congress, Wikimedia Commons, Archive.org,
West Virginia state records, and contemporaneous newspaper pages.
