   الأهمية: خطأ جوهري، وهو خطاف الفيديو وخاتمة أكبر مشاهده.

   الصواب: الثالثة على التوالي (2023: 0.99، 2024: 0.96، 2025: 0.96).
   الأهمية: خطأ في رقم.

3) الخطأ: "Forty of Japan's forty-seven prefectures are shrinking".
   الصواب: الرقم 40 هو عدد المحافظات التي سجّلت هجرة صافية خارجة منها. أما سكانها اليابانيون فتراجعوا في 46 من 47 محافظة (إحصاء 1 يناير 2026)، وطوكيو وحدها ارتفع عدد سكانها اليابانيين (+0.09%).
   الأهمية: خلط بين مقياسين.

4) الخطأ: "Every single year, Japan loses more than nine hundred thousand people".
   الصواب: الرقم 918,253 يخص 2025 فقط، وهو الفرق بين الوفيات والمواليد للمواطنين اليابانيين. والسكان الأجانب يعوضون جزءًا، فالإجمالي الفعلي انخفض نحو 563 ألفًا. وعبارة "city the size of San Francisco" صارت "bigger than San Francisco" لأن سان فرانسيسكو أصغر من 918 ألفًا.
   الأهمية: دقة.

   الصواب: 79,285 لم يكن رقمًا قياسيًا، بل ثالث ارتفاع سنوي متتالٍ. وفي 2025 تراجع الصافي إلى 65,219 (أول تراجع منذ أربع سنوات) وبقيت طوكيو الأولى بين المحافظات. أعتمد الآن بيانات 2025 كعنوان رئيسي.
   الأهمية: خطأ جوهري في تأطير البيانات.

6) الخطأ: "working-age population falls below sixty million by 2040, below fifty million by 2056".
   الصواب: حسب سيناريو IPSS المتوسط الرسمي: دون 60 مليونًا عام 2043، ودون 50 مليونًا عام 2062. الأرقام القديمة تقارب سيناريو الخصوبة المنخفضة (دون 50 مليونًا عام 2057).
   الأهمية: خطأ جوهري في الأرقام.

7) الخطأ: "the world's oldest pension system".
   الصواب: ليس أقدم نظام معاشات في العالم. المقصود مجتمع من أكبر المجتمعات سنًّا في العالم، فصارت "a pension system built for a much younger country".
   الأهمية: خطأ.

8) الخطأ: "Nine million homes… vacant" كأنها كلها مهجورة، و"the rest of Japan is EMPTY".
   الصواب: من 9.002 مليون وحدة شاغرة: 4.436 مليون للإيجار، و326 ألفًا للبيع، و384 ألفًا منازل ثانية، و3.856 مليون فقط مهجورة. وطوكيو نفسها فيها نحو 898 ألف وحدة شاغرة (10.9%). وعبارة "Japan doesn't have a housing shortage" صارت "Japan has more homes than households" (65.0 مليون وحدة مقابل 56.2 مليون أسرة).
   الأهمية: تضليل جوهري.

9) الخطأ: "two point one working-age Japanese supported each senior".
   الصواب: IPSS يحسب كل المقيمين بمن فيهم الأجانب، فصارت "working-age people".
   الأهمية: دقة.

10) الخطأ: خاتمة كوريا: "every one of these numbers… is worse" والشريحة "0.7".
   الصواب: ليست كل الأرقام أسوأ (مثلًا نسبة كبار السن في كوريا أقل، ومعدل الخصوبة هناك ارتفع في 2024 و2025). وأرقام الخصوبة أسوأ فعلًا: 0.80 عام 2025 (أدنى مستوى 0.72 عام 2023)، وسيول 0.63. صارت الصياغة والشريحة بهذه الأرقام.
   الأهمية: خطأ.

The lowest fertility in the entire country — and below one child per woman for the third year running. The highest? Okinawa — one point five two — the islands furthest from Tokyo.

VIS: two breathing population pyramids anchored over Tokyo and Okinawa on the map, morphing 1990→2025.

VO: So hold these two facts together. The young people are all flowing to the one place where people have the fewest children. Millions of future parents, walking into a city where parenthood goes to die. That's the paradox. And in twenty twenty-four, a private policy council finally gave it a name.

ACT 2 — THREE REASONS — 2:00–6:00
==================================================

Reason 1: The Space Squeeze — 2:00–3:20

VIS: SIGNATURE DIVE — camera plunges from national view into Tokyo, z-levels sharpening: Kanto → the 23 wards → Shinjuku rooftops. Choropleth rides the dive: ward-level reds deepening. Chip: "SHINJUKU — one of 16 'black hole' wards".

VO: Reason one: space. Dive into Tokyo and keep diving. This is where the national average goes to break apart — because averages hide the extremes. Central Tokyo packs people into some of Japan's most expensive square meters. Young couples squeeze into tiny apartments, with commutes that can eat nearly two hours a day. Now here's the strange part — zoom back out.

VIS: pull back to national view; 9.0M counter rises, then 3.86M of it highlights as "ABANDONED"; tiny house icons scatter across rural Japan, graying out.

VO: While Tokyo squeezes, much of the rest of Japan is emptying out. Nine million homes in Japan are vacant — nearly fourteen percent of all homes. About three point nine million of them are fully abandoned, and some sell for less than ten thousand dollars. Japan has more homes than households. What it lacks is people — everywhere except one city that's too expensive to raise kids in.

Reason 2: The Time Squeeze — 3:20–4:40

VIS: on the map over Tokyo: a clock-ring tightens around a worker icon; counter "AVG FIRST-TIME MOTHER: AGE 31". Line thread on map: marriages 489,119 ticking up slightly.

VO: Reason two: time. Tokyo runs on hours that leave nothing behind. Long workdays, long trains, and a career ladder that punishes anyone who steps off. The average first-time mother in Japan is now thirty-one years old. Marriage comes late — or not at all — and in Japan, where fewer than three percent of births happen outside marriage, late marriage IS low fertility. There's one flicker of hope: marriages actually rose last year, for the second year in a row — about four hundred eighty-nine thousand couples. Demographers are watching that number like hawks. But so far, it hasn't bent the curve.

Reason 3: The Machine Itself — 4:40–6:00

VIS: the mechanism animated: arrow pulls a young-woman icon from a rural town (town's pyramid loses its base), she lands in Tokyo (Tokyo's pyramid barely moves). Town after town grays out. Map wave: 744 municipalities flash red.

VO: Reason three — and this is the engine of the whole crisis: the migration machine eats twice. When a twenty-two-year-old woman leaves her hometown for Tokyo, her hometown loses her — and every child she might have had there. But Tokyo doesn't gain those children either… because Tokyo is where fertility drops below one. The births just vanish. Multiply that by tens of thousands of young people a year, for decades. In twenty twenty-four, Japan's Population Strategy Council — an independent group of business leaders and experts — ran the numbers on all one thousand seven hundred twenty-nine municipalities. Seven hundred forty-four of them — forty-three percent — are classified as "at risk of disappearing" by twenty fifty, meaning the number of young women there is projected to halve.

VIS: PAYOFF of cold-open tease: the council's report stamps "BLACK-HOLE MUNICIPALITIES — 25" onto the map; 16 Tokyo wards light up one by one: Shinjuku, Shibuya, Toshima…

VO: And the same report introduced a brand-new category — "black-hole municipalities." Places that suck in young people from across Japan, yet suppress the births that should follow. Twenty-five made the list. Sixteen of them… are Tokyo's own wards. That stamp you saw at the start? It comes straight from that report. Sixteen of Tokyo's twenty-three wards carry the label — given by that council, a private body, not the government. To be fair — some researchers criticize the council's method and its dramatic labels. Either way, the arithmetic is brutal.

==================================================

VIS: economy strip docks to map bottom (element 8): worker icons per retiree melt 2.1 → 1.3; dotted line (PROJECTION) of working-age population: 75M (2020) → under 60M by 2043 → under 50M by 2062.

VO: Now the bill. In twenty twenty, two point one working-age people supported each senior. By twenty seventy — on the government's own projections — that's one point three. Picture it: one worker, carrying one retiree, almost alone. The working-age population falls below sixty million by twenty forty-three, and below fifty million by twenty sixty-two. That means fewer taxpayers for a pension system built for a much younger country, fewer hands in factories and hospitals, and an economy that has to run faster every year… just to stand still.

VIS: GDP/pension threads strain; akiya houses multiply across the gray countryside.

VO: And those empty homes — nearly four million of them abandoned outright? They're what an economy looks like when it retreats — schools consolidating, train lines closing, entire towns handing out houses for free, hoping anyone young will stay. Japan isn't running out of money. It's running out of people to spend it on.
